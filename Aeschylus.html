Aeschylus (c. 525 - 456 BC)

Aeschylus was an ancient Greek playwright, often called the “Father of Tragedy.” He was born around 525 BCE, probably in Eleusis near Athens, during a period when Athenian democracy was developing and facing challenges from political conflicts and foreign invasions. He took part in the Greco-Persian Wars, fighting at the Battle of Marathon in 490 BCE and later at Artemisium and Salamis in 480 BCE. These experiences influenced his writing, especially his play Persians, which described the Persian invasion of Greece. The play was performed in 472 BCE and won first prize at the Great Dionysia, a major Athenian festival dedicated to Dionysus, the god of wine and theatre. At this festival, playwrights competed by presenting a series of tragedies followed by a satyr play, which was more humorous.

Throughout his career, Aeschylus wrote approximately 90 plays, although only seven tragedies have survived in their entirety. He won numerous dramatic competitions, with one ancient account crediting him with 13 first prizes. Although he once lost a competition to the younger playwright Sophocles in 468 BCE, he continued to achieve success, including with his Oedipus trilogy and his famous Oresteia trilogy, performed in 458 BCE. Aeschylus also travelled to Sicily, where he presented his plays at the court of Hieron I, ruler of Syracuse. He died at Gela, Sicily, in 456 or 455 BCE, and received a public funeral with dramatic performances held at his grave. His influence continued through his family, as his sons also became playwrights, and his work helped shape the development of Greek tragedy.

Famous Plays:

  • Oresteia - Ὀρέστεια (458 BC)
  • Agamemnon - Ἀγαμέμνων
  • The Persians - Πέρσαι (472 BC)
  • Seven Against Thebes - Ἑπτὰ ἐπὶ Θήβας [Hepta epi Thēbas] (467 BC)
  • The Suppliants - Ἱκέτιδες [Hiketides] (463 BC)
  • Prometheus Bound - Προμηθεὺς Δεσμώτης [Promētheús Desmṓtēs] (≃457 BC)
